Inventory of the Universe

  • The universe consists of the following objects:
    • Stars, which are organized into galaxies.
    • Planets, which orbit stars.
    • Moons, which orbit planets.
    • Debris, in the form of comets, asteroids, meteoroids.
    • Interstellar gas and dust.
    • Exotic objects, such as black holes, neutron stars, quasars, etc.

Our Cosmic Address

  • There are billions of billions of stars out there. Our Cosmic Address among these is as follows:
    • Our Solar System consists of our Sun and objects which orbit it, including the 8 planets.
    • Our Solar System is one among more than 100 billion star systems in the Milky Way Galaxy.
    • The Milky Way and the Andromeda Galaxy are the two largest out of about 40 galaxies in the Local Group.
    • The Local Group lies near the outskirts of the Local Supercluster.
    • The local Supercluster fades into the background of structure throughout the universe.
    • Altogether, there are about 100 billion galaxies in the visible universe.

Useful astronomical distance scales

  • A useful unit of distance in our Solar System is the AU (astronomical unit), defined as the average distance of Earth from the Sun.
    • This distance is about 150 million km.
    • In light-time, this distance equals 8.3 light-minutes.
  • Pluto is about 5 light-hours or 40 AU's away from the Sun.
  • The nearest star, Proxima Centauri, is 4.3 light-years away.
  • The Milky Way is about 100,000 light-years across and 1,000 light-years thick.
  • We are located about 2/3rds of the distance from center to edge or about 30,000 light-years.
  • In any direction, the edge of the visible universe is about 46 billion light-years away.
  • For the various techniques of measuring distance, see astrometry.

Speed and distance

  • A jet moves about 1 million times slower than light; a rocket moves about 27 thousand times slower.
  • At rocket speed, it would take about 100,000 years for us to travel to the nearest star (Proxima Centauri).
