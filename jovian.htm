Jovian moons

Galilean moons

  • The 4 large of moons of Jupiter originally observed by Galileo are known as Galilean moons.
  • Io
    • There are no impact craters on Io. The surface of Io may be younger than a million years old. It is continually being resurfaced by volcanic activity.
    • The surface composition on Io consists largely of sulfur, with deposits of frozen sulfur dioxide.
    Europa
    • Europa has the smoothest surface of any known solid object in the Solar System, with only a few large craters identified. This suggests that relatively recent events must have smoothed over the craters.
    • Icy surface may cover a subsurface water ocean, possibly kept liquid by tidal heating.
    • The ocean could conceivably harbor extraterrestrial life.
    Ganymede
    •  Largest satellite in the solar system. Larger than Mercury and Pluto, and only slightly smaller than Mars. It would easily be classified as a planet if were orbiting the Sun rather than Jupiter.
    • It is the only moon known to have its own internally generated magnetic field.
  • Callisto
    • The most heavily cratered object in the solar system.
    • A nearly complete absence of any geologic activity on its surface.
    • Thought to have evolved predominantly under the influence of impacts.
  • These differences are consequences of the different levels of tidal heating these moons experience.
    • Io is very hot because it is the nearest of the Galielean moons to Jupiter and has the most tidal heating.
    • Europa is farther and has more moderate tidal heating.
    • Tidal heating is even weaker on Ganymede.
    • Callisto is the most distant of the Galilean moons and does not experience significant tidal heating, which accounts for absence of geological activity.
  • The names of the Galilean moons derive from the lovers of Zeus (Jupiter).

 

Saturn's moons

  • Titan
    • This mammoth moon is the only moon in the solar system with a dense atmosphere.
    • The only world besides Earth that has standing bodies of liquid, including rivers, lakes and seas, on its surface. Like Earth, Titan's atmosphere is primarily nitrogen, along with a small amount of methane.

Jovian planets

  • Atmospheric methane is responsible for the blue color of Uranus and Neptune.
  • Typical wind speeds in Jupiter's atmosphere are much faster than hurrican speeds on Earth.
  • The Great Red Spot is a long-lived high-pressure storm on Jupiter.
  • Jupiter's magnetic field is about 20,000 times as strong as Earth's.
